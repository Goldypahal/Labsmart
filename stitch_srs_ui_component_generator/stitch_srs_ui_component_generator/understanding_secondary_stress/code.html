Understanding Secondary Traumatic Stress

psychology
Clinical Resource
Updated Oct 24, 2023

Secondary Traumatic Stress (STS) is the emotional duress that results when an individual hears about the firsthand traumatic experiences of another. For healthcare responders and clinicians, this is often an occupational hazard that mirrors the symptoms of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD).

While burnout is often related to workplace environment and workload, STS is specifically tied to the content of the clinical work itself. Understanding the nuances between these states is the first step toward building professional resilience and maintaining the high standard of care DiagConnect users deserve.

lightbulb

Key Takeaways

check_circle

Mirroring Symptoms

Symptoms often mimic PTSD, including intrusive thoughts and hyper-vigilance.

check_circle

Cumulative Impact

STS develops over time through repeated exposure to patient trauma narratives.

Actionable Steps for Responders

1

Establish "Micro-Transitions"

Create a 5-minute ritual between clinical sessions or before heading home. Use this time to physically acknowledge the end of the exposure period through deep breathing or a brief walk.

2

Scheduled Peer Debriefing

Engage in regular, structured conversations with colleagues. Sharing the emotional weight of clinical findings prevents isolation, which is a key driver of traumatic stress.

3

Cognitive Reframing

When distressing images arise, consciously pivot to the technical diagnostic success or the support provided. Focus on the 'helper' role you occupied during the event.
